## Who he is

His portfolio’s central idea is **Alive UI**: interfaces that change in response to a person’s behavior and progress rather than remaining visually static. MAWO, a habit-tracking product and case study, is the clearest expression of that theme. His public work also includes the Draftbook writing-assistant concept, a self-narrating neural-network preprint, smaller apps and browser games, books, and an audio-fiction series. [1] [2] [3]

## What the sources say

### Research and concepts

**Alive UI and MAWO.** Zenodo lists *Designing Alive UI in MAWO: A Case Study on Stimuli-Driven Interface Evolution for Habit Formation* as a preprint published 17 August 2025, authored by Vijayarka Nanduri. The paper describes a 45-day self-study using MAWO and reports habit completion rising from 71% to 96%, along with increased motivation and emotional engagement. This is a self-reported case study; present the percentage as a finding reported by that paper, not as proof of general effectiveness. The Zenodo record showed 302 views and 186 downloads when checked. [3]

MAWO’s GitHub README describes five visual phases, fragment-based progress, badges, offline use, local storage, and a Flutter/Dart implementation. The repository is marked MIT-licensed and links to an APK release and the research paper. The repository’s feature and privacy statements are project-owner claims; verify current implementation and release before repeating them as current facts. [4]

**Self-Narrating Neural Networks.** A second Zenodo preprint, dated 10 October 2025, proposes natural-language explanations of model training in real time. Its abstract describes an ECG-classification proof of concept involving two students and explicitly says the sample is too small for strong conclusions. Describe it as early-stage feasibility work, not a proven method or peer-reviewed result. [5]

**Draftbook.** The July 2025 Zenodo preprint discusses an AI writing assistant emphasizing quiet feedback, authorial control, and emotionally sensitive design. Crucially, its record contains a notice that the referenced Draftbook version is down and that an updated version is being worked on. The portfolio currently presents Draftbook as an available tool. Confirm the live status and update links and copy before launch. [6]

All three records identify the work as preprints on Zenodo. Use “preprint” or “research paper hosted on Zenodo” rather than implying peer review or journal publication unless further evidence confirms that status. [3] [5] [6]

## Positioning for the website

A strong site should make his distinctive through-line clear: **building human-centered software that changes how it behaves or feels as people use it**. Avoid presenting the work as an unconnected pile of apps, AI, books, and games. Use three main content pillars:

1. **Adaptive interfaces and behavioral design:** Alive UI, MAWO, and habit formation.
2. **Human-centered AI:** Draftbook, explainable ML, and interaction design around AI.
3. **Creative technology and writing:** games, books, guides, and audio fiction.

Suggested concise tagline: **“Human-centered interfaces, adaptive software, and ideas made tangible.”**

Suggested home-page intro:

> I’m Vijayarka Nanduri, an independent HCI researcher and product builder. I explore how software can adapt to people—not just collect their clicks. My work spans Alive UI, habit-forming tools, human-centered AI, and creative projects.

Suggested about-page paragraph:

> I work across research, design, and implementation. My current focus is Alive UI: interfaces that make progress visible and evolve with a person’s behavior. MAWO is my habit-tracking case study. Alongside interface research, I explore how AI tools can support creative work and make machine-learning processes easier to understand. I also write books and build games and other independent projects.

Treat both as draft copy for Vijayarka’s approval. If he prefers “AI researcher” or “HCI researcher,” use only the title he confirms and ensure the scope of experience is accurately represented.

## Recommended site map and content

**Home.** Clear name and positioning in the first screen, one sentence explaining the focus, and prominent links to About, Research, Projects, and Contact. Feature MAWO/Alive UI as the lead case study.

**About.** A first-person biography, working principles, current interests, and a short, dated timeline. Include only confirmed education, work history, location, and credentials. Add a good portrait only with his permission.

**Research.** One canonical page per work, with title, abstract in plain language, date, preprint status, DOI, downloadable paper, methods and limitations, and project links. Explain that the MAWO result comes from a 45-day self-study and the neural-network study involved two students.

**Projects.** Each project gets an individual case-study page, not only a card. State problem, intended users, his contribution, design decisions, implementation, privacy/data behavior, status (“live,” “prototype,” “archived”), screenshots, and link. Include a date last checked.

**Writing & media.** Books, guide, audio fiction, and selected videos with direct links and accurate availability labels.

**Contact.** A contact route chosen by him, plus professional profiles. Avoid exposing a personal email unless he approves; the current portfolio publicly lists one.

## SEO: priority implementation plan

### 1. Resolve accuracy and availability first

Audit every product link and mark it live, demo, archived, or in development. In particular, reconcile Draftbook with the Zenodo down notice. Verify the book listings, app downloads, and any performance or privacy statements. Correct the apparent paper-statistics discrepancy: the portfolio says 444+ combined reads and 408+ downloads across three papers, but the two accessible Zenodo records showed 302 + 94 = 396 views and 186 + 69 = 255 downloads at research time, before accounting for the third record and differences in Zenodo metrics. Do not reuse the site-wide numbers without refreshing and defining exactly what is counted. [1] [3] [5] [6]

### 2. Make the site crawlable and descriptive

Ensure each important page has its own crawlable URL and meaningful HTML text, including project descriptions that are not only embedded in images or client-rendered interactions. Use descriptive page titles and meta descriptions. Add canonical URLs, a sitemap.xml, robots.txt, social sharing metadata, and a custom 404 page. Use descriptive internal links such as “Read the MAWO research preprint,” not “click here.”

Suggested title tags (adapt to actual URL and length):

- Home: `Vijayarka Nanduri | HCI Researcher & Product Builder`
- About: `About Vijayarka Nanduri | Human-Centered Design and AI`
- Research: `Research by Vijayarka Nanduri | Alive UI, HCI & AI`
- MAWO: `MAWO: Adaptive Habit Tracker and Alive UI Case Study | Vijayarka Nanduri`
- Self-Narrating Neural Networks: `Self-Narrating Neural Networks | Vijayarka Nanduri`
- Work index: `Projects | Vijayarka Nanduri — Human-Centered Software`

Suggested home description: `Vijayarka Nanduri is an independent HCI researcher and product builder exploring adaptive interfaces, Alive UI, human-centered AI, and creative technology.`

### 3. Build specific, useful pages—not keyword repetitions

Prioritize content around genuine work and search intent: “Vijayarka Nanduri,” “Alive UI,” “MAWO habit tracker,” “adaptive interface design,” “human-centered AI writing tools,” and “self-narrating neural networks.” Explain terms naturally and show evidence. Do not claim “first,” “breakthrough,” “proven neuroscience,” or broad efficacy unless there is robust independent support. A detailed MAWO case study with screenshots and transparent method is more valuable than a generic SEO article.

### 4. Add structured data carefully

Use JSON-LD `Person` on the About page, with `name`, `url`, `image` (if approved), `sameAs` links to confirmed public profiles, and a concise `knowsAbout` list. Use `WebSite` for the site and `Article` or `ScholarlyArticle` on research pages only where fields are accurate. Do not label preprints as journal articles, invent organization affiliations, or add unverifiable alumni/employment facts. Google may use structured data to understand a page but does not guarantee enhanced search displays.

### 5. Strengthen author and research credibility

Link personal site ↔ GitHub ↔ Zenodo ↔ ORCID consistently. Use the exact author name and DOI on each research page. Publish short author-written summaries that link to the original preprint. Make limitations visible near findings. Include dates and project status. Add alt text to meaningful images and transcripts or descriptions for relevant video/audio.

### 6. Technical and measurement checks

Test mobile layout, page speed, keyboard navigation, heading hierarchy, image sizes, broken links, HTTPS, and rendered page text. Configure Google Search Console and submit the sitemap; verify ownership through an approved method. Use Bing Webmaster Tools if desired. Set up privacy-conscious analytics only with consent and suitable disclosure. Track branded queries and meaningful outcomes such as research-page visits and outbound paper clicks, not vanity traffic alone. Recheck index coverage after publishing; SEO indexing and ranking cannot be guaranteed.
